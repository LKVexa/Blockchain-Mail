What helps mail get accepted?

Your network needs outbound connections to mail servers on port 25. Residential and hosting networks sometimes block this port. The recipient’s server must support a verified secure connection using STARTTLS.

You can send from your local mailbox. Receiving servers decide whether to accept a local sender, and replies cannot reach a local address through the Internet.

A public domain you control is recommended for acceptance and replies. This build does not sign DKIM. Authorize your sending public IP in SPF and set matching forward/reverse DNS; recipients may still reject mail. These records are managed with your domain and network providers.

The message goes directly to the mail server found in the recipient domain’s DNS MX records. A receiving server may place accepted mail in spam. See Google’s sender requirements.
